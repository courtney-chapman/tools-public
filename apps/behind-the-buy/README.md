# Behind the Buy

**One purchase. Two workflows.**

Behind the Buy is a self-playing animation that shows how a U.S. Government prime contractor buys from a supplier. It follows one purchase through nine steps and, at each step, shows the records that support it. The point it makes is simple: issuing a purchase order is only the visible part of purchasing. The procurement file holds the evidence behind every decision.

It is an educational illustration, not a procurement tool. It doesn't assess a purchasing system, decide which rules apply or describe any company's procedures. Actual requirements depend on the prime contract, applicable regulations and company procedures.

## Watch it

Open `index.html` in a current browser. It is one self-contained file that runs offline: no server, account, fonts, analytics or network connection.

1. Choose one of the four purchases.
2. The animation plays on its own, about three minutes.
3. Use **Pause**, **Replay** or **All scenarios** below the animation. The space bar also pauses and resumes.

| Address option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `?s=<scenario id>` | Plays one scenario directly, for linking. |
| `?present` | Hides the buttons and footer, for screen recording. |
| `?t=<seconds>` | Freezes the animation at that moment, for checking a frame. |

If the computer is set to reduce motion, the animation opens on its finished frame. Replay plays it.

## Narration

All four scenarios are narrated; their cards on the start screen say "narrated." With the audio built in, `index.html` is about 6 MB. The voice follows the animation's own clock, so Pause, Replay and seeking stay in sync, and each part of the animation runs at least as long as its narration. Use **Mute** to turn the voice off. Browsers only play sound after a click, so if a narrated scenario is opened directly by its link, a **Turn on sound** button appears; click it to hear the narration. In recording mode (`?present`), a narrated scenario waits for one click before it starts, so the picture and the voice begin together.

## The four purchases

| Scenario | ID | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competitive commercial supply purchase | `competitive-commercial-supplies` | Several suppliers quote on commercial goods. Comparing their prices shows the price is fair. |
| Noncompetitive subcontract | `noncompetitive-subcontract` | Only one source can do the work. The file shows why, what alternatives were considered and how the price was proven fair. |
| Cost-reimbursement services subcontract | `cost-reimbursement-services` | The supplier is paid allowable costs plus a fixed fee. Cost realism, vouchers and final indirect rates come into play. |
| Subcontract involving Government property | `government-property` | Government test equipment goes to the supplier and must be authorized, recorded and accounted for at closeout. |

Every scenario follows the same nine steps: **Request, Solicit, Propose, Award, Deliver, Accept, Invoice, Pay, Close out**. Four actors take turns: the Requestor, the Buyer, the Supplier and Finance.

## How to read the records

Each step shows its records one at a time, then files them in that step's row of the procurement file. Every record is labeled one of three ways:

| On screen | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Solid card | Part of the purchase being illustrated. |
| **Conditional** (dashed) | Applies only to some purchases, for example a single-source purchase. |
| **Contract or policy** (outlined) | Depends on the prime contract's clauses or the company's purchasing procedures. |

These labels describe the illustrated purchase. They don't mean a record is required by regulation for every purchase, and the list for each step isn't complete.

## Public references

The record labels and classifications were checked against the public Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) and Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement (DFARS), which are available at [acquisition.gov](https://www.acquisition.gov/). The FAR is being rewritten, so section numbers may change; these were current in November 2026.

| Topic | References |
| --- | --- |
| Subcontracting policy, consent and notification | FAR Part 44; FAR 44.201-1, 44.402; FAR 52.244-2, 52.244-5, 52.244-6 |
| Contractor purchasing system | DFARS 252.244-7001; DFARS 244.402 |
| Make-or-buy programs | FAR 15.407-2 |
| Certified cost or pricing data and exceptions | FAR 15.403-1, 15.403-4, 15.406-2; FAR 52.215-12, 52.215-13 |
| Price, cost and cost realism analysis; technical evaluation | FAR 15.404-1, 15.404-3 |
| Fee limits | FAR 15.404-4 |
| Negotiation documentation | FAR 15.406-3 |
| Responsibility and exclusions | FAR 9.104-4, 9.405-2; FAR 52.209-6 |
| Small business subcontracting plans | FAR 52.219-9 |
| Cost-reimbursement contracts and payment | FAR 16.301-1, 16.304-1; FAR 31.201-2; FAR 52.216-7, 52.216-8; FAR 52.232-20, 52.232-22 |
| Audit and records retention | FAR 4.703; FAR 52.215-2 |
| Government property | FAR 45.202; FAR 52.245-1, 52.245-9; FAR 4.804-5 |

Many of these clauses apply to a subcontract only when the prime contract includes them and passes them down. That is why so many records are labeled conditional or contract-specific.

## Design and accessibility

- Uses a light color palette and sans serif titles.
- Who acts, the action and each record are shown in words and icons, never by color alone.
- Each record card stays on screen for about two seconds before the next appears.
- Buttons work from the keyboard and show a visible focus outline.
- The animation is designed for 16:9 landscape. On a phone it is readable in landscape.

## Limits

- Illustrative only. Not legal, regulatory or audit advice.
- The sequence is simplified. Real purchases can do steps in a different order, and other people can do some of the actions shown.
- Completing the steps shown doesn't make a purchasing system adequate or a purchase compliant.
- All scenarios, names and identifiers are fictional.
