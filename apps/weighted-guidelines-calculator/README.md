# Weighted Guidelines Profit Calculator

Built by Courtney. This offline browser calculator prepares a working prenegotiation profit or fee objective under the DoD Class Deviation 2026-O0048 Revision 1 structured weighted guidelines provisions. It is an analytical aid, not a completed DD Form 1547, a Government determination, an approved fee, or a negotiated price.

## Open and use

Extract the ZIP, then open `index.html` in a current browser. Keep `app.bundle.js`, `tokens.css`, and `styles.css` beside it. No account or network connection is needed for calculation. Optional source links require a connection if you choose to open them. Data stays in the current page and is not saved automatically. Downloaded files and browser PDF output remain wherever your browser saves them.

Choose your role and guidance level above the worksheet. The short guidance changes with each step for contracts, program, subcontracts, or finance; it does not change any number, rule, or calculation. The Government contracting officer selects weights and profit values for its objective. Other roles can supply or review facts, and contractors can model scenarios without making them the Government's conclusion.

Short single-choice questions appear in Topic / Your answer / Description rows or as compact choices. Use Tab to reach a choice and the arrow keys to move within a question. The selected choice has a checkmark as well as a highlighted border. Choose **Not sure** or **Not sure yet** when the fact is unresolved; those choices do not produce a completed objective. Contract and financing category remains a dropdown because it has many options.

1. **Start here:** Read the separate guidance for proposal preparers and Government evaluators. The contractor category starts at For-profit and the analysis method starts at Weighted guidelines; change either when the action differs, and verify both. Verify that the listed Revision 1 framework governs the action and that the negotiated-action and certified-data facts are established. Provide a verified DD Form 1547 Block 20 Government objective cost and its source. The tool does not select historical or later frameworks.
2. **Doing the work:** Decide how much of the performance-risk assessment belongs to technical uncertainty and how much belongs to management/cost control. Enter weights totaling 100%, then select a value for each area from its applicable range. Explain nonnormal or technology-incentive selections. A worked arithmetic example is available after the decision fields.
3. **Contract terms:** Choose the actual contract and financing category, separate incurred and remaining objective costs so they reconcile to Block 20, then select supported risk values. For progress payments, provide an independently verified Treasury Prompt Payment rate, its applicable period, and the contractor-financed share.
4. **Capital and savings:** Choose whether DD Form 1861-derived amounts are provided, qualifying capital is evidenced zero, or data is unavailable. Provided capital requires allocations, any supported adjustments, and an equipment value. Evidenced zero requires a written basis and no equipment value; the amounts section is hidden while zero is selected, and prior entries return if you switch back. Make an explicit cost-efficiency assessment. Unknown capital or unassessed efficiency prevents a final total.
5. **Review:** Calculate and inspect the block breakdown and expandable trace. Save named scenarios in the current session to compare objective dollars, percent changes, and changed inputs. Editing an input requires recalculation; saved scenarios remain in memory until the page closes.
6. **Export:** Inspect the print summary and use browser Print/Save PDF, CSV, or JSON. These outputs include limitations and source context; verify them for your action before use.

The contractor normally supplies the cost proposal, incurred and remaining cost support, financing facts, and DD Form 1861-derived capital data. The Government analyst or contracting officer verifies applicability and the objective cost base, assesses risk, selects weights and factor values, and documents the prenegotiation objective. A contractor can model a scenario without making those selections official. Open each **Required** decision section; closing it preserves the entries. Optional disclosures provide deeper guidance, and Review contains the full input and formula trace.

The fictional example is for checking navigation and arithmetic only. It does not prescribe selected factor values. Opening source links is optional; the calculator never sends entered data to them. The tool performs no file upload, account storage, analytics, or external calculation call. If inputs change after calculation, the prior result disappears until recalculated.

## Source and limits

Calculation method: [DoD Class Deviation 2026-O0048 Revision 1, DFARS 215](https://www.acq.osd.mil/dpap/dars/classdev/DFARS_RFO/Part-215/2026-O0048_Rev_1_TAB_A1_DFARS_215.docx) and [PGI 215](https://www.acq.osd.mil/dpap/dars/classdev/DFARS_RFO/Part-215/2026-O0048_Rev_1_TAB_A2_PGI_215.docx), effective July 22, 2026; DD Form 1547 reporting under PGI 253.215-70. The [DARS deviation index](https://www.acq.osd.mil/dpap/dars/dfars_far_overhaul_class_deviations.html) should be checked for later revisions. A progress-payment calculation requires a user-verified [Treasury Prompt Payment rate and period](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/payments-from-government/prompt-payment/rates).

The calculator supports the standard method and the identified modified nonprofit method. Cost-plus-award-fee, FFRDC, alternate structured approaches, unresolved applicability, and a sustaining-support nonprofit with fixed-price progress payments require action-specific review. It does not calculate DD Form 1861 from pools or annual cost-of-money rates. Factor selections, supporting evidence, and regulatory applicability remain with the user.

Reported block amounts round to whole dollars; reported Block 30 sums those rounded blocks. Exact rational decimal arithmetic is used before display rounding. Half-away-from-zero ties are a tool convention, not a claim about a mandated PGI rounding rule. The calculation trace and JSON distinguish raw and reported values.
