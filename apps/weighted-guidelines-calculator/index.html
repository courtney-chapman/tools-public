Step 1 of 6 · Start here

Before you calculate

DoD weighted guidelines combine several factors into a profit objective: a proposed profit amount used as a starting point in Government negotiation. This worksheet does not calculate the price a contractor should offer or the price the Government will agree to pay.

Who is this for, and what do I need?

Preparing a proposal?

Use the fictional example to learn the method. You can model assumptions, but your proposed price is not the Government's objective cost, and any risk values you enter are only a scenario unless the Government selects them.

Evaluating a proposal?

First establish the Government's objective cost and confirm this method applies. Then choose and document the weights and risk values using the proposal and other available evidence.

What you need before a real calculation

The governing rule for the action, an objective cost base, the contract and payment terms, supported risk selections, and facilities-capital information. If one is unknown, leave it unresolved; do not use the fictional example as a default.

Source framework

DoD Class Deviation 2026-O0048, Revision 1 (July 22, 2026). Check the DARS revision index before use. The app works offline; this source link is optional.

Step 2 of 6 · Performance risk

How difficult is the work to deliver?

Assess two challenges: meeting the technical requirements and managing the work. Give each a share of the assessment, then select its profit value.

Decision 1Divide the work risk between two areasRequired

A weight says how much each area counts in this part of the calculation. It is not a profit rate. Together, the two weights must add to 100%.

What should I consider when dividing the 100%?
  • Technical: uncertainty in designing, building, testing, or meeting the required performance.
  • Management and cost control: effort to coordinate the work, keep the schedule, and control spending.

The contracting officer assigns the relative weights for this particular action. There is no percentage that this calculator recommends.

Decision 2Choose the technical risk valueRequired

A value is the profit percentage selected for this area. The standard range is 3–7%, with 5% as its normal value. Consider the evidence about technical uncertainty; the calculator does not choose the value.

Decision 3Choose the management and cost-control valueRequired

This value reflects the effort and risk in managing the contract and controlling costs. The range is 3–7%, with 5% as normal. Consider the expected coordination, estimating reliability, internal controls, and ability to control cost and schedule.

Special case: timely proposal for an undefinitized action

When the qualifying-proposal conditions are met, the contracting officer may include the permitted management consideration in the selected value, up to 7%. This checkbox does not add a percentage point automatically.

See a worked example and the formula

Suppose the selected weights are 60% technical and 40% management. If the selected values are 5% and 4%, their contributions are 3.0 and 1.6 percentage points. Together they make a 4.6% performance-risk factor, which is multiplied by the Block 20 cost base. These numbers illustrate the math; they are not recommended selections.

Method: PGI 215.404-971(b). Modified nonprofit rule: PGI 215.404-972.

Step 3 of 6 · Contract type risk

How do the contract terms change risk?

Choose the actual contract and payment terms. Then separate costs already incurred at the qualifying proposal from costs to finish the work.

Decision 1Identify the deal and payment arrangementRequired

Use the contract type and financing terms for this action. The displayed normal value and range change with that choice; they are a starting framework for judgment, not an automatic selection.

Decision 2Separate completed work from remaining workRequired

The two amounts below must add to the Block 20 objective cost entered in Start here. If the action has no qualifying incurred-cost portion, enter an evidenced zero and put the full amount in cost to complete.

Decision 3Select the contract risk valuesRequired

A risk value is a profit percentage applied to each cost portion. The contracting officer chooses it from the supported range after considering the actual risk; the calculator does not choose it from the contract type.

Special case: substantial cost incurred before definitization

A documented undefinitized action may support a zero risk value in the circumstances described by the PGI. Do not use this exception merely because some cost was incurred.

Progress paymentsFinancing the workRequired

When the contractor finances part of the work before receiving progress payments, the method may add a working-capital amount. Supply the financed share, supported performance length, and Treasury Prompt Payment rate for the applicable period. The app does not look up or choose the rate.

Why is financing separate from contract risk?

Contract type risk reflects the risk from the deal and payment arrangement. Working capital is a separate calculation only for eligible fixed-price contracts with progress payments. The contractor may supply financing and schedule facts; the analyst verifies the bases, source rate, and assigned risk values. Performance-based payments alone do not activate this calculation.

Source: PGI 215.404-971(c); nonprofit sustaining-support modification: PGI 215.404-972(b)(2).

Step 4 of 6 · Capital and cost efficiency

What investment and savings count?

Equipment capital and supported cost reductions can affect profit. Use DD Form 1861-derived capital amounts; the app cannot derive them from a proposal total.

Facilities capital: land, buildings, and equipmentRequired

Zero capital is supported for this action. Record the evidence below; no equipment value or capital amounts are needed. If you switch back, your earlier entries will still be here.

Capital data is unavailable. The calculator will stop before a final objective; request the DD Form 1861-derived allocation or support for zero capital.

What should I take from DD Form 1861?

Use the action's supported capital-employed allocation among land, buildings, and equipment, with its source and date. This tool does not derive those amounts from CASB-CMF pools or cost-of-money rates. The contractor normally supplies the supporting capital data; the analyst verifies the allocation and selects the equipment factor. Keep formal investment-plan or intracompany-transfer additions separate and documented.

Cost efficiency: supported savingsRequired
When is a cost-efficiency increase supported?

Consider demonstrated, action-relevant reductions such as better methods, lower costs, or other efficiencies. The factor is a judgment-based increase, not an automatic reward or a score from this form. The contracting officer assesses and selects it; the contractor may provide evidence.

Who supplies these records?

The contractor normally supplies the capital records and evidence of cost reductions. The Government analyst verifies the allocation and decides whether an equipment value or cost-efficiency increase is supported. If capital data is unavailable, the app cannot finish the objective.

Sources: PGI 215.404-971(d)–(e); DD Form 1861.
