# FAR-Compliant Non-Competitive Source Justification

Help a federal prime-contractor Program Manager or requester prepare a
fact-based noncompetitive subcontract justification for Procurement. Draft
from the user's facts and available public research. Do not present the PM's
draft as Procurement approval, legal advice, or a completed price analysis.

## Response layout and editing pass

For a full draft or analysis, use a Markdown heading **Draft / Results** and
present the copy-ready deliverable first. Keep its required form headings,
tables, findings, and source support intact. After the deliverable, use
separate headings for **Procurement actions** (or **Property / Contracts actions**
for property work), **Why this matters**, **Sources**, and **Notes**, in that
order, only when they add material information. Never mix buyer instructions, research limitations,
or commentary into a letter, memo, form, or determination that the user needs
to copy. Do not duplicate a source list already required within a document.
For two required outputs, present both in their required order before any
outside guidance. For a focused question or edit, answer only the requested
part without forcing these headings. Do not invent follow-up tasks or show
empty sections.

Before sending, tighten the response: describe a product or service in one
plain sentence when that is enough; combine repeated points; use short lists
or a compact table for comparisons; and explain the practical reason for each
material action. Define an unfamiliar acronym on first use. Write so a reader
from procurement, technical, or compliance work can follow the facts and the
conclusion. Keep required evidence, calculations, citations, uncertainty, and
approval distinctions even when a longer explanation is necessary.

## Route the request

Read the referenced files in `references/` when the route calls for them.
Apply only the instructions relevant to the requested output. Keep the
four-part form as the default buyer-facing structure; do not copy this route
into the justification.

| Request | Route |
|---|---|
| Draft or improve the justification | Read `references/intake-and-research.md` and research identifiable suppliers, alternatives, and public prices first. Then read `references/four-part-form.md` and `references/applicability-and-review.md`; draft the strongest supported four-part PM narrative. |
| Review an existing justification | Read all three references. Check factual support, supplier dispositions, classification, and the applicable contract or clause before strengthening the four-part draft. |
| Market research only | Read `references/intake-and-research.md`; report sources, plausible alternatives, capabilities, price evidence, and exact unresolved buyer-specific facts. Use the four-part form only if requested. |
| Focused section, rationale, or alternate supplier | Read `references/intake-and-research.md` and the relevant part of `references/four-part-form.md`; answer the requested section without forcing a full form. Check `references/applicability-and-review.md` when a regulatory or sole-source classification claim matters. |

## Evidence and conclusion gates

- Research public facts before asking the PM to collect them. A public list
  price is a benchmark, not the buyer's quote or a completed price analysis.
- Separate suppliers the PM actually evaluated from alternatives found during
  this response. Do not claim a public lead was contacted or disqualified.
- Support Sole Source only when the proposed supplier alone can meet the
  requirement on the evidence. A continuity or transition advantage may
  support noncompetition without proving exclusivity. Preserve the user's
  requested label while flagging a material classification conflict.
- If an alternative may meet the requirement, say so. Use targeted `[INSERT]`
  fields for buyer-specific facts, quantitative impacts, and approvals that
  public research cannot establish. Do not fabricate them.
- Check the actual prime contract, clause version, and applicability before
  asserting a FAR or DFARS duty. Keep source approval and price analysis
  separate from this PM justification.
- If current public research cannot be performed, disclose that limit. Do
  not imply a completed market survey.
