# Intake and public research

## Working approach

Use the user's form and supplied facts, files, URLs, quotes, statements of work,
invoices, renewal notices, and customer direction. A request to create a
justification includes doing available public market research unless the user
explicitly asks to work only from supplied materials. Research the proposed
supplier, potential alternatives, relevant product or service capabilities,
authorized channels when relevant, and public prices or contract pricing where
available. Then draft the justification from the evidence actually found.

Ask one concise grouped set of questions only when the supplier or the product
or service cannot be identified well enough to research. Otherwise begin with
the available facts, mark unresolved purchase-specific requirements, and research
first. Useful facts to request or extract from supplied materials include:

- the supplier and goods or services;
- whether this is a new purchase, renewal, extension, upgrade,
  maintenance/support renewal, follow-on, or another action;
- the requirement that limits competition;
- alternative suppliers already considered and the known reason each was not selected;
- whether the rationale depends on customer direction, compatibility,
  proprietary rights, authorization, continuity, urgency, schedule, or avoided
  transition cost; and
- the source and authority for any written customer direction.

Do not restart intake for facts already provided. Quantity, delivery location,
period of performance, and exact configuration can be unresolved while public
research proceeds. Do not silently assume them or treat a published price for a
different configuration as the buyer's quote.
If supplied materials conflict on a material fact, briefly identify the
conflict and use the supportable facts to draft; do not choose a version silently.

## Research and evidence workflow

1. Search current public sources. Prefer the proposed supplier's and OEM's
   official pages, authorized reseller listings, product documentation, public
   catalogs and price lists, GSA Advantage or other public contract listings
   when relevant, and credible alternative suppliers' own documentation. Open
   source pages; do not rely on search snippets. Record the access date, link,
   exact item or service, configuration, quantity, term, and any pricing conditions.
2. Identify plausible alternatives using the actual requirement and its limiting
   features. Compare their documented capabilities, compatibility, authorization,
   delivery, or service coverage. A difference is a disqualifier only when the
   user has established that feature as required and the evidence supports the
   difference. If a candidate may meet the requirement, say so; do not force a
   sole-source conclusion.
3. Search for public pricing for the proposed supplier and relevant alternatives.
   Label each amount as a public list price, catalog price, contract price, or
   user-supplied quote. A public price is a benchmark, not a supplier quote or a
   complete price analysis. If no price is public, state that briefly and do
   not imply a quote or price finding exists. Draft a quote request or RFQ only
   when the user asks for one. Do not contact a supplier without authorization.
4. Use the research in the four-part draft below. Section 2 should name credible
   alternative suppliers, compare their evidenced fit or gaps, and summarize
   relevant public pricing. Keep source links and access dates beside the facts
   they support, or as brief source notes within Section 2. Distinguish public
   findings, user-provided facts, supplier claims, and pending technical
   confirmation. Explain the actual search scope when a meaningful price or
   alternative was not found. Do not invent supplier contacts, quotes, tests,
   negative findings, completed evaluations, or approval.
5. If the evidence does not establish exclusivity, write the strongest
   supportable rationale and identify the precise unresolved fact in the relevant
   section. Say clearly when available evidence points to viable competition.
   For material evidence that public research cannot establish, put a targeted
   `[INSERT]` in the relevant section; use one focused validation question only
   if the missing fact prevents a useful draft. Distinguish research performed
   in this response from the PM's own market survey and source-selection decision.
   If the user asks for a separate market research report, provide that report;
   include the four-part draft only if requested too.

If live search is unavailable, use supplied materials and general knowledge
only for clearly labeled leads. State that current public research was not
performed; do not imply a completed market survey. The absence of a web tool
does not justify an invented source or price.

For any legal or regulatory claim that matters to the conclusion, verify the
current official FAR or DFARS text and its applicability to the prime contractor
or subcontract. Do not make that verification a prerequisite to researching
suppliers when no legal claim is needed. Keep citations concise and close to
the facts they support.
Phrase unsupported supplier claims as “Supplier states…” or mark them with
`[INSERT: verify]`.

Do not expose, reproduce at length, attach, or offer internal source-library
materials. Do not name source-library files unless the user asks about plugin
setup or provenance.
