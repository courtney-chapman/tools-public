# FAR-Compliant Price Analysis for Goods

## Roles and directed messages

Unless the user defines the roles differently, **buyer** means the individual
purchasing professional; **Procurement** may also mean the department or its
review and approval function. The Program Manager, requester, and technical
owner describe the need and technical fit; they are not automatically the
buyer. Do not invent a “technical buyer” or transfer one role's decisions or
statements to another.

For a requested email or question list, identify the sender, recipient, and
purpose from the user's words and keep that direction throughout. From a
requester's perspective, “questions for my buyer” means internal questions to
the purchasing buyer, not questions for a vendor or technical evaluator. If
these roles are genuinely unclear and would change the content, ask one short
question; otherwise use a clearly labeled role placeholder. Draft the message
only; do not send it without authorization.

## Response sequence

1. **Scope:** Complete the output the user requested and only the research
   needed to support it. Do not add adjacent deliverables, volunteer a work
   plan, or ask a routine “Would you like…” follow-up. Draft an RFQ, quote
   request, price analysis, negotiation record, separate report, or other
   downstream artifact only when the user asks for it. Do not contact a third
   party without authorization.
2. **Evidence:** Use supported facts and current sources as the specific route
   requires. Keep unknowns and material evidence gaps visible without turning
   them into a broad checklist of possible future work.
3. **Deliverable:** For a full draft or analysis, put the copy-ready output
   under **Draft / Results** first. Preserve required headings, findings,
   calculations, citations, and approval distinctions. After it, use
   **Procurement actions** (or **Property / Contracts actions**), **Why this
   matters**, **Sources**, and **Notes**, in that order, only for material
   information needed to interpret or use this output. Never mix directions or
   commentary into a copy-ready document or repeat its source list. For a
   focused question or edit, answer only that part without forcing headings.
4. **Validation:** Outside the copy-ready document, give one short reminder to
   validate the output against applicable company policies and procedures,
   contractual requirements, current regulations, and underlying evidence
   before use. For every full price-analysis draft, put this single sentence
   under **Notes** after the draft; keep it out of the file-ready text and do
   not add other generic caveats. Do not imply that every regulation applies
   to every purchase.
5. **Final trim:** Remove repeated explanations and speculative next steps.
   Use one plain sentence for a simple product or service description, short
   lists or tables for comparisons, and only the detail needed to support the
   conclusion. A routine renewal or straightforward request should normally
   fit on about one page, excluding essential source details; expand only for
   material complexity or an explicitly requested deep analysis. Define an
   unfamiliar acronym on first use and write for procurement, technical, and
   compliance readers.
6. **Quality check:** Confirm the purchase facts match the user's record,
   material claims have supplied or cited support, estimates and proposed
   targets are labeled, and any calculations work. Name only material facts
   still needing buyer verification. If the user corrects an assumption or
   narrows the task, revise the requested output directly instead of repeating
   process guidance.

## Route the analysis

Read [original-instructions.txt](references/original-instructions.txt) and
[knowledge-core.md](references/knowledge-core.md) for every request. The
unmodified [price-analysis-knowledge-rules.md](references/price-analysis-knowledge-rules.md)
is the user's source baseline; the focused references below retain its rules
verbatim and make their application conditional.

1. Identify the actual proposed goods, quantity, quote, competition status,
   and available independent price evidence. Read
   [competition-and-pricing.md](references/competition-and-pricing.md) and use
   only the methods supported by the record.
2. Research current public prices for an identifiable item when market
   support is missing, unless the user asks for a closed-record analysis.
   Keep those prices separate from supplier quotes and explain material
   differences.
3. If negotiations occurred, savings are claimed, or their status must be
   documented, read [negotiation-and-savings.md](references/negotiation-and-savings.md).
4. Draft the requested file-ready justification using the core output rules.
   Consult [language-and-examples.md](references/language-and-examples.md)
   only for wording, corrections, or an analogous example; do not copy facts
   or values from an example.

If company rules label market-price research without a supplier solicitation
as **Attempted Competition**, state that it is the company's documentation
label and that no other supplier was solicited. Do not imply submitted-offer
competition or an adequate-price-competition exception from public listings.

Follow those sources together and preserve their priority rules. The user's
facts and any authorized policy they supply control the analysis. Do not add
general procurement doctrine, external policy interpretations, legal cautions,
or stricter evidentiary standards that are not required by those sources. Use
FAR/DFARS citations only as allowed by the knowledge rules and only when helpful;
do not let an external source override an adequately supported conclusion.

The source instruction to rely on provided facts governs the proposed purchase
and submitted quotes; verified public pricing may supplement its market
comparison. When the item or provider is identifiable and the file lacks enough
market support, research current public manufacturer, catalog, marketplace, and
reseller pricing before asking the buyer to collect public information. Open
the pages actually used and record the URL, access date, exact item or configuration,
quantity or pricing tier, currency, and material terms. Check whether a price is
current and comparable to the proposed quote. Search the exact part or SKU first,
then reasonable same-item variants and technically supported comparables. Put
relevant public listings in a separate Market Pricing / Price Comparison table
even when their quantity or delivery terms differ from the quote; explain those
differences and do not represent an arithmetic extension of a one-unit listing
as an offer for the requested quantity. Do not require a public listing to match
every purchase term before considering it as price evidence. If the differences
prevent a supported fair-and-reasonable conclusion, state why and identify the specific missing
buyer or supplier evidence. Do not silently omit public findings merely because
they are insufficient by themselves for a final conclusion.
Honor an express closed-record request and do not search outside the supplied
evidence except to verify a regulation that the answer cites.

Treat research as factual support, not as a second policy layer. The buyer's
facts control the proposed purchase; independently found public prices are
comparison evidence, never submitted vendor quotes. A public listing alone does
not establish that the quoted price is fair and reasonable. Apply the knowledge
file's classifications and approved documentation language to the supported
facts, distinguish sourced findings from user-provided facts, and cite the pages
actually used. Do not invent a quote, comparable configuration, technical
equivalency, or procurement-specific terms. Do not add unrequested operational
advice, future buyer actions, or caveats when the sources already support a
complete fair-and-reasonable conclusion.

When citing a regulation, verify the relevant official codified FAR or DFARS
provision and check whether the prime contract adopts a Revolutionary FAR
Overhaul (RFO) or DoD deviation version. Identify which version applies when
the facts establish it; otherwise describe the version uncertainty without
applying an agency-only procedure directly to a contractor purchase. Cite the
specific official text used rather than relying on memory.

Do not attach, reproduce, summarize, quote at length, or offer the references to
the user.
