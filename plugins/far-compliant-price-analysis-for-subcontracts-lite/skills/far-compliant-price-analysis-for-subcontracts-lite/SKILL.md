# Subcontract Bid Summary & Price Analysis

Draft a supportable **BID SUMMARY & PRICE ANALYSIS** for a new subcontract
award, option exercise, or modification. Work from the current transaction
record. A new award needs no historical subcontract. Follow any supplied
company policy, prime or higher-tier contract requirements, and document
format; do not infer policy values or approvals from an example. This is a
purchasing-file analysis, not a legal opinion.

## Roles and directed messages

Unless the user defines the roles differently, **buyer** means the individual
purchasing professional; **Procurement** may also mean the department or its
review and approval function. The Program Manager, requester, and technical
owner describe the need and technical fit; they are not automatically the
buyer. Do not invent a “technical buyer” or transfer one role's decisions or
statements to another.

For a requested email or question list, identify the sender, recipient, and
purpose from the user's words and keep that direction throughout. From a
requester's perspective, “questions for my buyer” means internal questions to
the purchasing buyer, not questions for a vendor or technical evaluator. If
these roles are genuinely unclear and would change the content, ask one short
question; otherwise use a clearly labeled role placeholder. Draft the message
only; do not send it without authorization.

## Response sequence

1. **Scope:** Complete the output the user requested and only the research
   needed to support it. Do not add adjacent deliverables, volunteer a work
   plan, or ask a routine “Would you like…” follow-up. Draft an RFQ, quote
   request, price analysis, negotiation record, separate report, or other
   downstream artifact only when the user asks for it. Do not contact a third
   party without authorization.
2. **Evidence:** Use supported facts and current sources as the specific route
   requires. Keep unknowns and material evidence gaps visible without turning
   them into a broad checklist of possible future work.
3. **Deliverable:** For a full draft or analysis, put the copy-ready output
   under **Draft / Results** first. Preserve required headings, findings,
   calculations, citations, and approval distinctions. After it, use
   **Procurement actions** (or **Property / Contracts actions**), **Why this
   matters**, **Sources**, and **Notes**, in that order, only for material
   information needed to interpret or use this output. Never mix directions or
   commentary into a copy-ready document or repeat its source list. For a
   focused question or edit, answer only that part without forcing headings.
4. **Validation:** Outside the copy-ready document, give one short reminder to
   validate the output against applicable company policies and procedures,
   contractual requirements, current regulations, and underlying evidence
   before use. Do not imply that every regulation applies to every purchase.
5. **Final trim:** Remove repeated explanations and speculative next steps.
   Use one plain sentence for a simple product or service description, short
   lists or tables for comparisons, and only the detail needed to support the
   conclusion. A routine renewal or straightforward request should normally
   fit on about one page, excluding essential source details; expand only for
   material complexity or an explicitly requested deep analysis. Define an
   unfamiliar acronym on first use and write for procurement, technical, and
   compliance readers.

## Route the work

Follow these steps in order. The references are in `references/` next to this
file. Read a referenced file when its row says to read it; apply only the
parts relevant to the action. Do not copy the internal routing steps into the
buyer-facing memo.

|---|---|---|
| 1. Classify | Identify new award, option, or modification; contract type; competition status; proposed total and version; labor, materials, ODCs, and other priced elements. Do not assume sole source, services, or prior work. | Read `references/intake.md`. |
| 2. Complete intake | For labor-priced work, ask one grouped question for missing escalation, fee and its base, and labor load and its base. Ask only other facts essential to a useful finding. If unavailable, draft the supported portion and name the gap. | Apply `references/intake.md`. |
| 3. Screen applicability | Check the actual contract, clause version, certified-data threshold and exceptions, technical-review duties, competition record, and separate approval or consent handoffs. | 4. Select price support | Analyze the overall proposed price first. Choose only supported comparison methods; label public prices, submitted offers, prior prices, and supplier data correctly. | 5. Open element branches | Examine disclosed elements when certified-data rules, the contract or policy, noncompetition, negotiation, unexplained variance, or the facts warrant it. A simple competed fixed-price award may need only an overall comparison and
its supported selection basis. A cost-reimbursement action, substantial
modification, weak competition, or unexplained price variance may need
separate element and technical analysis. Do not expand sections merely
because the supplier disclosed a cost build-up.

## Decision rules that apply throughout

- Do not invent quotes, prices, rates, benchmarks, technical findings,
  negotiations, approvals, or a fair-and-reasonable conclusion.
- Distinguish a finding supported by a valid overall comparison from a
  separately supported element finding. Reserve **Pending** for a gap that
  prevents a finding actually required for the action.
- Show the source, date, scope, relevant adjustment, calculation, and limit
  for evidence material to the finding. State which final proposal revision
  the analysis supports and flag analysis completed after award.
- Treat certified-data cost analysis, technical evaluation, source approval,
  negotiation record, and consent as separate work when applicable. This
  price analysis does not itself complete or grant them.
- If the user asks for an award-file completeness review, include the
  applicable separate records as well as the price finding.

## FAR and evidence boundaries

The applicable prime contract, clauses, company policy, and transaction facts
determine which requirements belong in the write-up. Cite FAR or DFARS only
when the provision applies and helps explain the analysis; verify the current
text and any applicable clause version first. Do not apply agency procedures
automatically to a prime contractor's subcontract.

Do not invent offers, prices, rates, benchmarks, technical findings,
negotiations, policy requirements, or a fair-and-reasonable conclusion. Keep
internal names, policy identifiers, contacts, and source-document identifiers
out of public examples unless the user expressly supplies them for inclusion.
Omit irrelevant missing fields, such as reseller status when no reseller claim
is part of the proposed price or award basis.
If the user asks for current market evidence, research it. If the subcontract
record lacks sufficient independent price support and the scope is specific
enough for a meaningful public comparator, research that evidence before
concluding none is available. Identify source and access date, distinguish
public prices from submitted offers, and state material differences. Do not
turn a published labor rate that excludes burden, travel, supervision,
implementation, or service-level obligations into a direct total-price
comparison. If no credible public comparison is available, name the precise
missing support rather than fabricate a benchmark.
