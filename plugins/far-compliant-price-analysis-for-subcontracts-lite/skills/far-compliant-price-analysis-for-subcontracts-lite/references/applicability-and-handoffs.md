# Applicability and file handoffs

Check the applicable prime or higher-tier contract, clause version (including
any deviation or Alternate I), and supplied company rules before asserting a
requirement. Screen the award or modification for certified cost or pricing
data: relevant award date and rule version, tier, action amount, adjustment
amount where applicable, and any competition, commercial-item, waiver, or
other exception. Do not assume a threshold, exception, or company approval
rule. If certified data are required, route the element-by-element cost analysis
and overall price analysis required by FAR 15.404-1 to the appropriate process;
this price-analysis draft alone does not satisfy that duty. If price analysis
is insufficient without certified data, identify the additional data or cost
analysis needed. When the analysis supports a higher-tier price proposal,
make its results suitable for inclusion in that proposal (FAR 15.404-3(b)).
Do not list an unknown award date or rule version as a material gap when the
action is clearly below every potentially applicable certified-data threshold
and no other fact indicates certified data are needed.
For a modification, test the applicable certified-data threshold using the
aggregate of price increases and decreases, not the net price change, under
FAR 15.403-4(a)(1)(iii). Show the gross increase, gross decrease, and aggregate
when the line-level changes are available. State the threshold screen is
unresolved if only a net adjustment is supplied and the distinction could
change the result; still check applicable exceptions and the contract's rule
version.
When FAR 52.244-5 is included in the prime contract, note its direction to
select subcontractors competitively to the maximum practical extent
consistent with the contract. Record the actual competition history or
identify the separate noncompetitive sourcing rationale where relevant; do
not treat the price analysis as that rationale.

When DFARS 252.244-7001 **Basic** (or its applicable deviation) governs,
check whether the record contains a timely, adequate cost or price analysis
**and technical evaluation for each supplier proposal or quote** under
paragraph (c)(10), plus the applicable purchasing-system procedures under
(c)(22). Alternate I deletes these paragraphs; do not apply Basic by default.
Use the actual technical evaluation or identify the specific missing review.
Do not invent a Program Manager's finding. Consider adjacent Basic requirements
as relevant: vendor evaluation (c)(8), management justification and cost or
price analysis for sole/single-source awards (c)(9), purchase history (c)(5),
negotiation documentation (c)(11), discounts (c)(12), contract type (c)(13),
and modification rationale (c)(15). Flag a missing separate record or
handoff; do not fabricate those records inside the price analysis.
For each applicable handoff, give a concise status outside the formal price
memo: present with a record reference, pending with an owner or next action,
not applicable with a reason, or unknown. Include sole/single-source approval
or management justification, negotiation and discount records, and consent to
subcontract when applicable. Do not state or imply that completing the price
analysis supplies an approval or consent.
When negotiations occurred, check whether the separate record identifies the
initial proposal, buyer's supported objective or position if one was developed,
final negotiated amount, and reasons for material changes or concessions.
Identify a missing record or fact; do not create a negotiation history from
the difference between two prices.

If asked whether the entire award file is complete, flag any separately
applicable certified-data cost analysis, technical evaluation, negotiation
record, consent, subcontract-type rationale, or file documentation. When
consent to subcontract applies, the supporting cost or price analysis is
among the matters reviewed under FAR 44.202-2(a)(8); identify the separate
consent package or handoff rather than implying this write-up grants consent.
Do not add those records as routine sections of this BID SUMMARY.

Beyond the three service-pricing inputs above, ask for missing facts only when
they prevent a useful analysis. If enough facts exist to draft part of the
document, write that part and identify the specific gap. Do not request
administrative fields, labor hours, or historical files by default. Request a
technical evaluation when an applicable clause or policy requires one or when
a technical finding is needed to support the analysis.
