# Action and evidence intake

## Start with the action and evidence

Identify the action, requirement, supplier or competing suppliers, proposed
amount, pricing structure, periods, and award basis. Use the stated contract
type and competition status. Do not assume the action is sole source, for
services, or a continuation of prior work.
For services, identify the measurable scope available: proposed hours or
outputs, labor mix, work location, performance period, material service levels,
and deliverables. Use a buyer-developed estimate when one exists; record its
date, assumptions, and whether it preceded receipt or review of supplier
pricing. Never create a retrospective estimate and call it independent.

For a service or other labor-priced subcontract, **before drafting the final
analysis**, ask one grouped question for any of these three pricing inputs not
already supplied:

1. Proposed escalation by year or period, or confirmation that no escalation
   is proposed or that it is not applicable. If period rates are supplied,
   calculate the escalation instead of asking for a percentage.
2. Separately proposed fee or profit amount or percentage **and its cost base**,
   or confirmation that fee is included in the price/rates or not separately
   disclosed.
3. Labor load or burden: fringe, overhead, G&A, or an overall multiplier and
   the base to which it applies, or confirmation that only fully burdened
   rates are available and the build-up is not separately disclosed.

Ask only for missing items, in one short prompt, alongside any other fact
essential to the requested analysis. If the user says an input is unavailable,
included, or not applicable, accept that answer and draft with the available
evidence. Do not repeatedly ask or infer 3% escalation, 7% fee, a 2.4 load,
or any other standard value. Do not ask for a labor build-up when the
subcontract has no labor-priced component. A missing breakout can limit a
separate element finding without preventing a supported overall price finding.
