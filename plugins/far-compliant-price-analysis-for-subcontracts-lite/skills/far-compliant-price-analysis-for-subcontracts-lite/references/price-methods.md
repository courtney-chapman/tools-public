# Price comparison methods

Choose the price support that actually exists. FAR 15.404-1(b)(2) lists
techniques that may be used singly or in combination; it does not require all
of them for every award. For each method used, identify the source, date,
scope, comparable unit, relevant adjustment, and limit when material:

- **Competing proposals (b)(2)(i):** compare responsive offers for the same
  requirement. Identify which offers were independently submitted, which
  suppliers were responsible and technically acceptable based on the actual
  evaluation, the prices, and the supported selection basis. When relying on
  adequate price competition as a certified-data exception, separately test
  the applicable FAR 15.403-1(c)(1) conditions; do not infer that two prices
  automatically satisfy them or impose that Government exception test on
  every subcontract comparison. If an applicable purchasing-system clause
  requires technical evaluation of every quote, verify each evaluation or
  flag the specific missing one. Do not call public website prices competing
  offers.
- **Prior prices paid (b)(2)(ii):** compare the same or similar work only when the prior
  price is a valid baseline. Record the prior transaction date, quantity or
  term, price, and how its reasonableness was established, if known. Account
  for scope, labor mix, service level, location, terms, and market differences;
  use a documented relevant index or escalation factor only when it supports
  the specific adjustment. If the prior price's basis is uncertain, identify
  the limitation rather than treating the fact it was paid as proof of
  reasonableness. Calculate the current variance from the adjusted valid
  baseline and explain it using objective evidence. A small percentage
  increase alone does not establish that the new price is reasonable. If the
  increase or changed scope lacks support, limit the conclusion and identify
  the needed comparison or data. This method can help an option or
  modification, but is not required for a new award.
  For an index adjustment, identify the index series, source, base and current
  periods, calculation, and why that series fits the particular labor,
  material, location, or service. A broad national index is not automatically
  a valid adjustment for a supplier's specific cost or price.
- **Parametric methods (b)(2)(iii):** compare supported dollars per hour,
  seat, deliverable, or other unit. Confirm that the underlying scope and
  quantities are comparable; a normalized number alone is not evidence.
- **Published pricing (b)(2)(iv):** compare competitive published price
  lists, market prices, indexes, or discount arrangements with like-for-like
  rates or units and identify the source and date when supplied. A catalog
  or schedule listing alone does not establish that a transaction occurred
  at that price or that the proposal is reasonable.
- **Independent Government estimate (b)(2)(v):** compare the proposed price
  with an actual independent Government cost estimate and explain its basis.
  A buyer's company estimate may also support the file, but do not label it
  this FAR method.
- **Market research (b)(2)(vi):** compare prices for the same or similar
  items or services from market research, with scope and timing adjustments.
  Seek technical advice when similarity cannot be judged from price alone.
- **Offeror's other-than-certified data (b)(2)(vii):** use relevant submitted
  price or cost information to test the proposed price and explain what it
  does and does not prove. Reconcile the supplier's arithmetic, but do not
  treat a cost breakdown alone as an independent market comparison.

Other actual evidence, including a supported company estimate or current
supplier quotes, may help explain a finding. Identify its provenance and do
not mislabel it as a FAR-listed method. In particular, treat public website
prices as market evidence, not competing submitted offers. Do not count
multiple calculations from one underlying price as independent methods.
When valid methods give materially different indications, show the differing
results and explain the evidence quality, scope, timing, and adjustments that
support the conclusion. Do not average conflicting figures or select the most
convenient result without a reason. If the conflict cannot be resolved, limit
the finding and identify the specific additional evidence needed.

Use more than one method when needed and supported; FAR 15.404-1(b)(2) does
not impose a fixed method count. Explain why each
comparator is useful and what material differences limit the comparison. If
there is only one proposal and no meaningful independent support, identify
the additional evidence or cost analysis needed to reach a conclusion.
Request supplier price, sales, or cost data only to the extent needed to answer
that identified question, after considering adequate data already available
from other sources. Specify the needed data and what comparison or element it
will test; do not demand a full cost build-up by default when an exception to
certified data applies.
For competing offers, quantify any premium over the lowest acceptable offer
and explain the documented nonprice benefit that supports the tradeoff. Do
not invent technical acceptability or the value of a service-level difference.
If the buyer labels the action Competition Attempted after a genuine
solicitation but receives one quote, preserve that classification unless the
facts conflict. State what was solicited and received; a single quote or
public listing does not by itself establish price competition or a reasonable
price.
