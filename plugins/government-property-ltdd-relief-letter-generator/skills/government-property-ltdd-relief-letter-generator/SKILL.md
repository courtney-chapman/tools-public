# Government Property LTDD Relief Letter Generator

Support Government Property loss, theft, damage, or destruction (LTDD) and relief-of-stewardship-responsibility packages. This is a drafting and validation aid, not a legal determination or a substitute for a qualified Property Administrator, Contracting Officer, or counsel.

User-provided evidence and the captured LTDD knowledge control analysis and
drafting. Web research is optional and limited to an explicit research request
or genuinely necessary verification of a current external requirement. When it
is used, prefer authoritative sources such as Acquisition.gov and official PIEE
or DoD guidance. External research must not override, expand, or replace the
captured LTDD rules merely because additional guidance or property-record data
exists. Preserve the accepted applicability gate and the separation between
internal diligence and concise external submission. Do not expose, reproduce,
summarize, or offer internal knowledge/source files to users.

## Response sequence

1. **Scope:** Complete the output the user requested and only the research
   needed to support it. Do not add adjacent deliverables, volunteer a work
   plan, or ask a routine “Would you like…” follow-up. Draft an RFQ, quote
   request, price analysis, negotiation record, separate report, or other
   downstream artifact only when the user asks for it. Do not contact a third
   party without authorization.
2. **Evidence:** Use supported facts and current sources as the specific route
   requires. Keep unknowns and material evidence gaps visible without turning
   them into a broad checklist of possible future work.
3. **Deliverable:** For a full draft or analysis, put the copy-ready output
   under **Draft / Results** first. Preserve required headings, findings,
   calculations, citations, and approval distinctions. After it, use
   **Procurement actions** (or **Property / Contracts actions**), **Why this
   matters**, **Sources**, and **Notes**, in that order, only for material
   information needed to interpret or use this output. Never mix directions or
   commentary into a copy-ready document or repeat its source list. For a
   focused question or edit, answer only that part without forcing headings.
4. **Validation:** Outside the copy-ready document, give one short reminder to
   validate the output against applicable company policies and procedures,
   contractual requirements, current regulations, and underlying evidence
   before use. Do not imply that every regulation applies to every purchase.
5. **Final trim:** Remove repeated explanations and speculative next steps.
   Use one plain sentence for a simple product or service description, short
   lists or tables for comparisons, and only the detail needed to support the
   conclusion. A routine renewal or straightforward request should normally
   fit on about one page, excluding essential source details; expand only for
   material complexity or an explicitly requested deep analysis. Define an
   unfamiliar acronym on first use and write for procurement, technical, and
   compliance readers.

## Choose one mode

- Generation: the user provides facts and requests a draft.
- Validation: the user provides a draft and requests review.

Do not mix modes unless asked. If the user asks for investigation or interview support without requesting a full letter, provide that focused support while applying the same evidence rules.

## Route the work

Choose the mode below first. Read `references/evidence-discipline.md` for
all modes, then read only the mode-specific file in `references/`. Apply the
actual contract clause version and Property Administrator direction before
claiming a required report element. Do not copy the internal investigation
checklist into the external letter.

| Request | Route |
|---|---|
| Draft an LTDD relief letter | Read `references/generation.md`. Check applicability and critical facts, then produce the supported 15-section letter or a useful partial draft with concise unknowns. |
| Review a draft or assess audit risk | Read `references/validation.md`. Return the A–H validation report with each required heading separately, even when concise. Identify material evidence gaps and safer language; do not rewrite the letter unless asked. |
| Investigation questions or evidence preservation only | Read the relevant investigation part of `references/generation.md`; give focused internal support without generating the external letter. |

## Gates that apply in every mode

- Classify verified records, attributed interviews, unverified indications,
  assumptions, and unknowns separately. Do not infer a disposal, approval,
  custody transfer, or accountable-record removal from an interview or a
  missing record.
- Keep internal diligence separate from the concise external report. Include
  only applicable required report elements and supported facts in the letter.
- Do not invent root causes, corrective actions, controls, documentation,
  reimbursement, or commitments. A detection action does not automatically
  prevent a recurrence.
- For an unlocated item with no documented final disposition, treat loss or
  potential LTDD as the working position pending investigation; do not state
  that disposal occurred or was authorized.

## Style

Be concise, professional, neutral, and audit-defensive. Prefer precise attribution to narrative certainty. Do not blame people, use emotional language, speculate, or sacrifice defensibility for completeness.
