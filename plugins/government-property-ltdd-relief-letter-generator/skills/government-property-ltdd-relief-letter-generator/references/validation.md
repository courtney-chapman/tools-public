# LTDD validation

## Validation output

Return a validation report only; do not rewrite the full letter unless asked.
Keep every A–H heading, but scale the detail to the draft and evidence supplied.
For a short or incomplete draft, group related missing report elements in one
row or sentence instead of expanding the entire checklist. Assess the complete
submission gate without treating an absent fact as proof that a conditional
requirement applies. Use `unknown / verify applicability` where the contract,
property type, or Property Administrator direction is not supplied. Avoid
repeating the same issue under multiple headings; give its consequence once,
then cross-reference it briefly if needed. For a one- or two-sentence draft,
aim for roughly 250–350 words across A–H unless distinct required findings
need more. Use one sentence each in C–F when that fully states the issue; use
no more than the decisive records in G. Do not add a pass row solely to restate
a supplied fact. Use:

A. PASS / FAIL — result and concise reason

B. Required Elements Check — table with `Requirement | Status | Issue | Recommended Fix`. Include only failed or unresolved requirement groups and any decisive pass. Group routine omissions in a partial draft; expand rows when a distinct requirement, contradiction, or cited evidence warrants it.

C. Evidence vs. Assumption Issues — identify what is supportable, what is unsupported, and safer wording

D. Timeline / Consistency Issues

E. Root Cause Adequacy

F. Corrective Action Adequacy

For each action, test whether it addresses the supported control failure and
would prevent recurrence, or only detect a later discrepancy. Flag a detection-
only action when it does not address the cause. Do not prescribe an unsupported
new control as though it has been implemented.

G. Supporting Documentation Gaps — prioritize the documents that would resolve
the actual claims and the applicable submission requirements. For a sparse
case, name the few decisive records or record groups; do not inventory every
possible investigation source.

H. Risky Language + Safer Alternative — quote each material risky phrase and give source-consistent replacement wording

Flag unsupported claims about disposition, authorization, approval, handling, custody, movement, use, or accountable-record removal. Identify conflicting dates, last-inventory gaps, storage/access contradictions, commingling conflicts, duplicate sections, and incorrect citations.

Fail validation when a material requirement is missing or unsupported, including:

- the company-specific financial-liability statement;
- willful-misconduct statement or relief request;
- the required property-record and report elements, or supporting record covering them, for the applicable clause and Property Administrator direction;
- current or future need and last known location;
- sensitive/classified/hazardous/toxic/export-controlled and related notification status, or Ground Flight Risk statement;
- consistent dates and commingling treatment, with no duplicate or incorrect Ground Flight Risk statement;
- evidence for a claimed disposal, destruction, wiping, cannibalization, repurposing, movement, authorization, or use;
- a control-based root cause responsive to the facts;
- specific corrective actions mapped to causes with a supported preventive mechanism; or
- supporting documents aligned with key claims.

Flag these major DCMA/audit risks when the facts or draft raise them:
unsupported disposal language, missing authorization chain or final-disposition
evidence for a claimed event, weak custody trail, hazardous-battery
documentation gaps for an indicated battery or hazardous-handling issue, and
blame-based or vague root-cause labels. Do not introduce battery, flight-risk,
classified, export-control, or similar hypothetical scenarios into the review
merely because they appear on the applicability checklist. Where status is
required but unknown, state the status gap briefly without inventing a hazard.

For a short draft, focus the report on the decisive defect, the safe working
position, and the minimum evidence or language needed to resolve it. Keep
necessary missing submission elements visible as grouped gaps. Investigation
questions must arise from the supplied facts; do not ask broad hypothetical
questions or propose controls when no cause is established.

End with a safe working position that states what can presently be concluded without overstating the evidence. For unlocated property with interview-only disposal narratives, the safe position is potential LTDD or loss pending investigation and evidence validation, with no concluded disposal or authorization.

Clearly separate internal diligence from required letter changes. Additional questions or evidence gaps may be identified for resolution before submission, but recommend adding them to the external LTDD only when needed to satisfy an applicable requirement or support the concise factual account.
