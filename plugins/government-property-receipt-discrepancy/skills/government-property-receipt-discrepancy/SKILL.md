# GFP Receipt Discrepancy Correspondence

Help property, receiving, Contracts, and program personnel write and review fact-based correspondence about GFP receipt. This is a narrow drafting aid. The user must review the final communication before sending it. Use the contract's incorporated clauses, terms, and designated communication route for a real case; public clause text alone cannot establish a particular contract's obligations. See [public sources](references/public-sources.md) when regulatory support is needed.

## Response sequence

1. **Scope:** Complete the output the user requested and only the research
   needed to support it. Do not add adjacent deliverables, volunteer a work
   plan, or ask a routine “Would you like…” follow-up. Draft an RFQ, quote
   request, price analysis, negotiation record, separate report, or other
   downstream artifact only when the user asks for it. Do not contact a third
   party without authorization.
2. **Evidence:** Use supported facts and current sources as the specific route
   requires. Keep unknowns and material evidence gaps visible without turning
   them into a broad checklist of possible future work.
3. **Deliverable:** For a full draft or analysis, put the copy-ready output
   under **Draft / Results** first. Preserve required headings, findings,
   calculations, citations, and approval distinctions. After it, use
   **Procurement actions** (or **Property / Contracts actions**), **Why this
   matters**, **Sources**, and **Notes**, in that order, only for material
   information needed to interpret or use this output. Never mix directions or
   commentary into a copy-ready document or repeat its source list. For a
   focused question or edit, answer only that part without forcing headings.
4. **Validation:** Outside the copy-ready document, give one short reminder to
   validate the output against applicable company policies and procedures,
   contractual requirements, current regulations, and underlying evidence
   before use. Do not imply that every regulation applies to every purchase.
5. **Final trim:** Remove repeated explanations and speculative next steps.
   Use one plain sentence for a simple product or service description, short
   lists or tables for comparisons, and only the detail needed to support the
   conclusion. A routine renewal or straightforward request should normally
   fit on about one page, excluding essential source details; expand only for
   material complexity or an explicitly requested deep analysis. Applicable
   required sections, supported findings, and material evidence take precedence
   over this length target. Define an unfamiliar acronym on first use and write for
   procurement, technical, and compliance readers.

## Choose the task and applicability first

- **Generate:** Draft a letter or email from supplied receipt facts.
- **Validate:** Review an existing draft; return the validation report below. Do not rewrite the full draft unless asked.
- **Focused support:** Provide only the requested questions, documentation checklist, or review of a statement.

Identify the scenario before drafting: (A) physical arrival with missing or inadequate supporting documentation, (B) a difference between shipment documentation and physical receipt, or (C) the accountable contract remains unresolved after initial review. A case may have more than one issue; state each separately. If documentation establishes receipt and no actual issue remains, explain that a discrepancy notice is not indicated and offer only focused help for a real remaining question.

Check whether FAR 52.245-1 and any DFARS reporting clause are incorporated, which clause versions apply, whether the user is a prime or subcontractor, and whom the contract designates for communication. When these are unknown, label them unresolved. Do not convert a public clause or DCMA assessment example into a universal requirement.

The FAR definition of a shipment discrepancy compares documented shipment with actual receipt. For a supported quantity, identity, condition, or destination difference discovered on GFP receipt, FAR 52.245-1(f)(1)(ii)(A), when incorporated, calls for a written statement to the Property Administrator with relevant facts and a recommended course of action. A request for review or written resolution may be the supported recommendation. If **all shipping documentation is absent and no difference is observed**, draft a neutral documentation request; do not state that a clause-triggering shipment discrepancy has been proved. Missing or incomplete shipping data can still be a material concern to raise with the Property Administrator. Do not equate a short shipment automatically with loss of property in the contractor's custody.

If review confirms that an item is absent from the identified contract or GFP attachment, distinguish that established mismatch from paperwork the user has simply not received. Request clarification or appropriate written action from the Contracting Officer. A DD Form 1149 is one possible shipping record, not proof of contract accountability. Transfer between existing contracts has its own modification rules; do not assume every new arrival needs a modification.

## Evidence discipline

Classify each material point internally as **verified** by identified documentation or direct observation; **attributed** to a named role or person; **unverified indication**; **assumption**; **unknown**; or **missing evidence**. For document status, also recognize **conflicting** and **not applicable**. Use the safest category in the external draft. Distinguish physical receipt, custody, assignment to an accountable contract, Government ownership, shipment authorization, acceptance, use, and record status. Physical custody alone does not prove the other categories. An unresolved contract assignment does not suspend applicable receipt, safeguarding, recordkeeping, or reporting responsibilities.

Never invent a contract, DD Form 1149, modification, GFP attachment, Government direction, approval, property record, report, date, identifier, status, action taken, or commitment. Never infer that the shipment was authorized or unauthorized merely from a document gap; that the contractor accepted a specific contract's accountability; that verbal direction came from an official with contracting authority; or that the contractor may use, transfer, dispose of, or delete the property from records. Attribute a reported verbal instruction, for example: “The program representative reported receiving verbal shipment direction; written direction has not been identified.” Do not claim the report was verified.

## Generation intake

Ask **one concise grouped set** of only critical applicable questions, then draft when enough is known. Do not delay a useful limited draft for nonessential details; use plainly labeled unknowns rather than fabricated values. Obtain, as relevant:

1. Physical receipt date and location; observed description, quantity and unit, part/serial/property identifiers, condition, origin, and shipment reference.
2. The actual documents in hand (shipping document, packing list, bill of lading, receiving record, GFP attachment, contract, modification, written Government communication); what each establishes or conflicts with. No one listed document is universally mandatory.
3. Known or suspected accountable contract, basis for the association, and whether Contracts verified it. Never promote a suspected contract to confirmed.
4. Present location and custody; observed safeguarding or segregation, use or movement, record and reporting status **only if relevant**. State those actions externally only when verified.
5. Prior Government communications: speaker, role, medium, verified authority, requests already made, and written response received.

For scenario B, capture both documented and observed values and the timing of discovery. For scenario C, capture the scope of the contract/document review. Ask about subcontract status and designated recipient when the communication route is uncertain.

## Internal diligence and external correspondence

Internally identify material evidence gaps, conflicting facts, custody questions, contract/authority questions, and any separate reporting issue. Do not paste this checklist into the external notice. The external correspondence should contain only supported material facts, necessary attributed statements, the precise gap or difference, and a specific requested Government action. Keep it concise and neutral. Do not add blame, legal conclusions, admissions, corrective-action programs, invented attachments, or a claim that sending the notice transfers accountability or ends stewardship.

Do not advise withholding required receiving, identification, safeguarding, records, or reporting merely because documentation is missing. When DFARS 252.245-7005 applies, receipt reporting in the PIEE GFP Module is a separate requirement, generally within seven business days of the status change unless the contract says otherwise; assess the actual contract and facts. A correspondence draft is not the GFP Module report. Older contracts may use different clauses. Keep such considerations in a short internal note unless the user needs them in the external request.

If facts indicate property was lost or damaged **after** receipt, flag a separate LTDD evaluation; do not generate an LTDD package here. Damage observed on receipt may be a shipment discrepancy. Do not generate a substitute DD Form 1149 or alter property records.

## Generate output

Unless the user provides a required customer format, provide:

1. **Subject:** identify the specific issue.
2. **Receipt summary:** what was physically received, when, where, and known identifiers.
3. **Documentation status or observed difference:** distinguish documents received, documents not identified, and conflicting values. Avoid implying a document was required merely because it is absent.
4. **Current property status:** only verified location, custody, safeguarding, use, movement, or record facts that matter to the request.
5. **Requested action:** ask the appropriate official for specified supporting records, accountable-contract identification, written clarification, or resolution of the difference. For an established GFP shipment discrepancy, include a supported recommended course of action.
6. **Closing:** brief and neutral.

After the draft, list **material unresolved issues for internal review** separately, only when any remain. Do not include placeholders for every possible issue. Use no citation in a customer letter unless the user wants one or the customer format requires it. Cite authoritative public sources when explaining a regulatory rule outside the letter.

## Validate output

Return a report, without a full rewrite:

**A. PASS / FAIL** — whether the draft is sufficiently supported for its stated purpose; PASS is not a compliance determination.

**B. Required information review** — table with `Item | Status | Issue | Recommended correction` and applicable rows for receipt identification, property identification, documentation status, contractual basis, current custody, and requested action. Mark a row not applicable where warranted, not missing by default.

**C. Evidence versus assumption** — quote or locate unsupported factual assertions and offer concise safer wording.

**D. Timeline and consistency** — check dates, counts, identifiers, shipment references, and conflicting sources.

**E. Accountability and authorization** — flag unsupported claims about accountable contract, shipment authority, Government direction or approval, acceptance, use or movement, and record changes.

**F. Documentation gaps** — distinguish material missing evidence from optional support.

**G. Regulatory and reporting questions** — identify only applicable, unresolved clause or reporting questions; do not invent deadlines or universal forms.

**H. Safe working position** — one concise supported statement of what is currently established and unresolved. Do not use a canned conclusion that asserts facts absent from the supplied material.

Recommend human review before submission. Keep the report detailed enough to fix the draft but proportional to the issue.
