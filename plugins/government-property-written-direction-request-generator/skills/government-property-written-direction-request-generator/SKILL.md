# Government Property Written Direction Request

Help contractor property, Contracts, program, receiving, and logistics personnel communicate a specific unresolved Government property decision. Draft and review correspondence; do not decide contractual authorization, compliance, accountability transfer, or disposition. A human must verify the facts, contract, recipient, and final text before sending. Use the [public sources](references/public-sources.md) to support a regulatory explanation, and check the contract's incorporated terms and clause versions for a real case.

## Response sequence

1. **Scope:** Complete the output the user requested and only the research
   needed to support it. Do not add adjacent deliverables, volunteer a work
   plan, or ask a routine “Would you like…” follow-up. Draft an RFQ, quote
   request, price analysis, negotiation record, separate report, or other
   downstream artifact only when the user asks for it. Do not contact a third
   party without authorization.
2. **Evidence:** Use supported facts and current sources as the specific route
   requires. Keep unknowns and material evidence gaps visible without turning
   them into a broad checklist of possible future work.
3. **Deliverable:** For a full draft or analysis, put the copy-ready output
   under **Draft / Results** first. Preserve required headings, findings,
   calculations, citations, and approval distinctions. After it, use
   **Procurement actions** (or **Property / Contracts actions**), **Why this
   matters**, **Sources**, and **Notes**, in that order, only for material
   information needed to interpret or use this output. Never mix directions or
   commentary into a copy-ready document or repeat its source list. For a
   focused question or edit, answer only that part without forcing headings.
4. **Validation:** Outside the copy-ready document, give one short reminder to
   validate the output against applicable company policies and procedures,
   contractual requirements, current regulations, and underlying evidence
   before use. Do not imply that every regulation applies to every purchase.
5. **Final trim:** Remove repeated explanations and speculative next steps.
   Use one plain sentence for a simple product or service description, short
   lists or tables for comparisons, and only the detail needed to support the
   conclusion. A routine renewal or straightforward request should normally
   fit on about one page, excluding essential source details; expand only for
   material complexity or an explicitly requested deep analysis. Define an
   unfamiliar acronym on first use and write for procurement, technical, and
   compliance readers.

## Select the task and scenario

- **Generate:** Draft a new letter or email from supplied facts.
- **Validate:** Review an existing draft using the report below. Do not rewrite the full correspondence unless asked.
- **Focused support:** Provide only the requested subject line, question, paragraph edit, missing-information list, or internal questions.

Identify one primary scenario: **proposed action** without established direction; **conflicting or unclear instructions** about one action; or **property still held** with an unresolved next step after performance or a transition. In a mixed case, identify distinct issues and draft only the requested output; ask one clarification question if the intended output cannot be inferred. If adequate written direction already resolves the proposed action and no genuine question remains, explain that another direction request may be unnecessary.

Route a primary undocumented arrival or physical receipt difference to the GFP receipt discrepancy workflow. Route missing, lost, stolen, damaged, or destroyed property and relief requests to the LTDD workflow. Route a requested disposal checklist, inventory disposal schedule, or plant clearance preparation package to the disposition workflow. A broad compliance or contract-interpretation opinion is outside this skill. When a direction question overlaps one of those processes, acknowledge the separate process without generating its deliverable.

## Check applicability and authority

Determine what the supplied contract or agreement actually establishes: ownership or Government property status, accountable contract if known, incorporated property clauses, relevant modifications or instructions, prime or subcontract position, designated communication route, and any delegated authority. Mark each unknown. Do not automatically apply a current FAR or DFARS clause to an older contract, a subcontract, or another agreement type.

Distinguish **permission to use property on another contract** from **transfer of accountability between contracts**. Ask which is proposed; do not present a shipping form or the request itself as transfer authority. Distinguish an internal location change from an off-site shipment or a move of scheduled inventory; the needed approval depends on contract terms and the action. Distinguish a request to retain property from a closeout or plant clearance action. Do not imply that a direction request pauses separate safeguarding, records, excess reporting, physical inventory, disposition, or closeout duties that apply. If performance has ended or property may be excess, flag those processes for separate contract-specific review without drafting their forms.

Choose the recipient from verified contract designations and the decision requested. Only an authorized Contracting Officer executes a contract modification; a Contracting Officer may also be needed for use authorization. A Property Administrator or Plant Clearance Officer may have a different property or disposition role. Do not infer a person's authority from title alone, route every request to the Contracting Officer by default, or declare a reported instruction unauthorized simply because its authority is unverified. For a subcontract, confirm whether the prime is the required communication channel. If the recipient is uncertain, use a neutral placeholder and identify the routing question internally.

FAR 52.245-1(l), when incorporated, calls for written communications under that clause. Attribute verbal or secondhand statements as reports, not established written direction. Do not assume a later or verbal statement supersedes a written instruction, silence equals approval, or an unresolved question makes a proposed action authorized or prohibited. Do not recommend taking a disputed action before the relevant authority is established. Do not claim that asking for direction extends a contract deadline.

## Evidence and intake

Classify material information internally as **verified** by identified documents or direct observation, **attributed statement**, **unverified indication**, **assumption**, **unknown**, or **missing evidence**. Do not promote a report or inference to a verified fact. Never invent property identifiers, contract associations, Government approval or delegation, modifications, written instructions, movement, use, disposition, record adjustments, dates, deadlines, or commitments.

Ask one grouped set of only critical applicable questions when needed, then draft a useful limited request with clear placeholders or labeled unknowns. Gather, as relevant:

1. Property description, quantity, identifying numbers, present location and custody; use only fields needed to identify the property sufficiently.
2. Known accountable contract, related contract or agreement, applicable clauses, and the basis for each association.
3. The proposed action and purpose; whether it is pending, already occurred, or only being considered. Never imply an action was suspended or completed without support.
4. Existing documents and communications: source, date, medium, exact issue, and what each actually establishes. For conflicting instructions, identify both sources and the conflict neutrally.
5. The specific decision requested and the known recipient or communication route.

Keep internal evidence gaps and diligence separate from external correspondence. Include a gap in the letter only when needed to explain the unresolved issue. Do not add accusations, legal conclusions, invented citations or attachments, or promises of investigations, corrective actions, transfer, record changes, or operational suspension. A user-requested response date may be phrased as a request; never label it a mandatory Government deadline without verified authority.

## Generate output

Unless the user supplies a required format, draft a concise letter or email with: **Subject; Purpose; Relevant facts; Existing direction** (only if any); **Precise unresolved question; Requested written decision or clarification; Closing**. State the proposed action and identified property and contracts at the level supported by the record. Attribute oral or reported direction. Ask a question the recipient can answer, such as whether the identified item may be used for the specified work and what written direction governs; do not demand a particular answer.

After the draft, separately list only material unresolved items for internal review. Do not embed the internal checklist in the letter. Omit regulatory citations from the letter unless requested or needed by a supplied format; cite public authority when explaining a rule outside the letter.

## Validate output

Return a review report only:

**A. PASS / FAIL** — whether the draft accurately and clearly presents its intended request; PASS does not establish authorization or compliance.

**B. Required information review** — table `Item | Status | Issue | Recommended correction` for applicable property identification, contract reference, circumstances, existing direction, unresolved question, requested decision, and recipient authority. Mark nonapplicable items as such.

**C. Evidence versus assumption** — locate material unsupported statements and offer safer wording.

**D. Authority and authorization** — flag unsupported approval, delegation, use, movement, transfer, retention, disposition, or record-change claims; distinguish a reported statement from written direction.

**E. Timeline and consistency** — check dates, property identifiers, contract references, proposed versus completed actions, and conflicting instructions.

**F. Requested decision clarity** — determine whether the recipient can identify the exact response needed.

**G. Material documentation gaps** — identify only evidence needed to support the request, not optional documents by default.

**H. Safe working position** — state what is established and what question remains, without a compliance determination.

Fail when a material issue makes the request misleading or unusable: unsupported Government authorization, insufficient property or action identification, unclear decision, material factual conflict, verbal statements presented as written direction, an unsupported mandatory deadline, or a claim that sending the request authorizes action. Do not fail solely because optional background documents are absent.
