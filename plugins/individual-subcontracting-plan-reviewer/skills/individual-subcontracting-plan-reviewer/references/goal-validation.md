# Goal and period validation

Work from the supplied worksheet, plan, and governing instructions. Do not change entered amounts. Show calculations with the source period and denominator; distinguish **blank/unknown** from an entered zero. Parse currency only when unambiguous. Negative, nonnumeric, and precision-conflicting amounts are findings, not values to silently repair. Use exact cents or adequate decimal precision for arithmetic; state rounding.

## Per-period checks

1. Identify each actual base and option period, and its planned contract dollars if supplied. Do not assume four options or an option value.
2. For each period and category, combine direct projected subcontract dollars with allocated indirect dollars **only if** the user says indirect costs are included in goals and supplies a usable allocation basis. Otherwise leave the disputed amount unresolved. Check that direct and indirect entries do not duplicate the same work.
3. Calculate total planned subcontract dollars from the distinct **Small Business** and **Large Business** buckets (`SB + LB`), provided both are known and consistently classified. The six socioeconomic goals overlap; never add SDB, WOSB, HUBZone, VOSB, or SDVOSB to derive total SB.
4. Calculate each category's subcontract-dollar goal percentage as `category goal dollars ÷ total planned subcontract dollars × 100`. If the denominator is blank or zero, report **not calculable**, not 0%. If the contracting officer requires contract-dollar goals, calculate them separately as `category goal dollars ÷ applicable contract dollars × 100`; do not replace the subcontract-dollar basis.
5. A category percentage above 100%, or category dollars above total planned subcontracts, needs a basis and reconciliation. Compare SDVOSB with VOSB and SB where the underlying projected awards are the same, and other socioeconomic categories with SB where ordinary classification applies. An ANC/Indian-tribe statutory credit or other clause-specific counting rule can change apparent subset relationships: flag it for review instead of automatically declaring a violation. A single opportunity may count in multiple eligible categories without being added twice to total SB.
6. An entered zero is an explicit proposed goal. Review whether the solicitation or clause requires an explanation; otherwise ask for the supporting rationale only if the zero materially affects the assessment. A blank is missing information, not a zero goal.

## Across periods and documents

- Sum dollars across base and actual options to obtain contract-wide totals for each category, planned subcontracts, and contract value. Contract-wide goal percentage is `sum(category dollars) ÷ sum(relevant denominator dollars) × 100`; **do not average period percentages**.
- Verify period labels and dates, missing options, duplicated amounts, and direct/indirect consistency. Compare final plan figures with goal worksheets and opportunity projections. Identify both documents and exact fields when they conflict; do not choose one without authorization.
- If the supplied plan treats an option as unexercised, do not drop its **planned** goals from the individual-plan draft merely because it is not yet exercised; distinguish plan goals from the later ISR's cumulative reporting rules.
- Review whether the principal supplies/services and dollar opportunities plausibly support proposed goals. Supplier research, historical performance, and planned outreach support different kinds of claims; none establishes an actual future award.
