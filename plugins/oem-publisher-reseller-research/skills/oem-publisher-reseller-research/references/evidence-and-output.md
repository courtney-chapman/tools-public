# Evidence and output standards

Prefer current, directly relevant primary sources: maker or publisher sites, official partner directories, product and licensing documentation, program terms, announcements, and authoritative corporate records. Secondary sources include distributor and reseller pages, marketplaces, contract-vehicle listings, and credible independent reporting. A contract-vehicle listing shows a sales route on that vehicle; it does not independently prove manufacturer authorization. Open cited pages and support each material relationship or restriction with the specific page, not a general homepage or search snippet.

Do not use or quote a document marked CUI, classified, export-controlled, or otherwise access-restricted merely because a public site exposes it. Do not treat an aggregator's AI summary of such a document as evidence. Seek ordinary public maker, partner, supplier, or contract-channel sources instead. If an underlying primary source cannot be safely reviewed, leave the claimed relationship `Not Verified` rather than assigning an authoritative classification.

Classify each material finding, not an entire organization:

| Classification | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| Manufacturer/Publisher Verified | An official maker or publisher source directly supports the finding. |
| Authoritatively Verified | A controlling filing or public record supports the finding. |
| Independently Corroborated | A credible source independent of the claimant supports it. |
| Supplier Claimed | The interested supplier states it without independent verification. |
| Historical | Dated evidence does not establish current status. |
| Conflicting Evidence | Relevant sources materially disagree. |
| Not Verified | Public evidence reviewed is insufficient. |

Distinguish `not found in the reviewed sources`, `not publicly disclosed`, `not verified`, and `contradicted by authoritative evidence`. Do not turn absence of a search result into a negative fact. State the search or source limit when it matters. Put source organization, direct link, publication or effective date when available, and access date near the finding or in a compact source list. For live research, use the actual access date; never invent source dates.

Start with the direct answer. For several organizations, use only useful columns from organization, role, product or market scope, relationship, authorization status, classification, and source. When distribution is central, use a short text map that distinguishes verified and unverified edges. For requested channel comparisons, identify seller, documented upstream source, licensing or product authority, customer restrictions, and support implications where known. Omit repeated empty columns. Keep targeted verification questions to unresolved facts that would affect the buyer's question; do not replace available public research with a question list.

Before finalizing, check that maker and publisher identity, each role, authorization scope, every upstream edge, exclusivity, product mapping, ownership, and channel implication match their sources. Mark historical and conflicting evidence. Avoid a procurement determination or unrequested follow-on document.
