# Roles and distribution channels

Identify the organization that actually manufactures, develops, publishes, licenses, owns, or commercially controls the named offering. A brand, parent, contract manufacturer, publisher, ISV, SaaS provider, and cloud host can be different entities. Record acquisitions, transfers, white labels, or rebrands only when relevant and sourced. Do not equate the marketer or seller with the original maker.

Interpret OEM in the transaction's context: it may mean the maker or brand owner of the item the buyer wants, or an equipment builder that buys a component for incorporation into its own product. State which role the evidence supports rather than assuming that every OEM is the buyer's direct sales channel.

Classify each organization by evidenced role and scope. Useful roles include OEM, contract manufacturer, brand owner, software publisher, ISV, SaaS or cloud provider, master or public-sector distributor, distributor, authorized or independent reseller, value-added reseller (VAR), systems integrator, implementation partner, managed or authorized service provider, licensing partner, marketplace operator, sales agent, and broker. Roles are not mutually exclusive. Attach product or service, territory, customer segment, and period when sources permit; do not give a company a universal role based on one offering.

When the buyer asks how an offering reaches customers, who can supply it, or what channel options exist, look across relevant direct and indirect routes rather than limiting discovery to resellers. Surface several documented or potential contacts where evidence permits: the maker's direct sales channel, distributors, resellers and VARs, systems integrators, marketplaces, and sales agents or referral partners. Include a channel participant with documented brand or product-family involvement even if no exact-model listing is found; the buyer can use an RFQ to investigate availability. For each organization, distinguish an apparent direct RFQ seller from an upstream distributor or introducer, and distinguish exact-model listings, broader product-family or brand evidence, and maker-verified authorization. Do not present an upstream distributor and its downstream reseller as independent sources without noting their documented relationship. Keep the search representative and proportional; do not imply the list is exhaustive or decide whether the buyer has adequate competition.

Trace only documented edges: direct sale, OEM or publisher to distributor, distributor to reseller, marketplace seller to customer, licensing, fulfillment, hosting, entitlement, implementation, support, and renewal. For software, distinguish the seller or invoice issuer from the rights grantor, subscription controller, host, implementer, and support provider. A partnership or shared ecosystem does not prove that a particular reseller obtains stock or licenses through a particular distributor, and a general supply relationship does not prove a transaction's fulfillment route.

When relevant, distinguish government agency, prime contractor, subcontractor, education, nonprofit, and commercial channels. A channel open to an agency may not be open to a contractor. Check deployment model, product line, licensing tier, territory, and renewal status. Identify direct purchase separately from direct support, licensing, fulfillment, and invoicing. Compare alternative channels for the same offering without adding substitute products unless asked.

For a relationship map, draw an edge only when a source supports that edge and its scope. Mark supplier-claimed or unverified edges explicitly. If only parallel participation in a partner network is established, show separate nodes rather than an invented upstream link.
