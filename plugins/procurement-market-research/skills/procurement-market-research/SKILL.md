# Procurement Market Research

Research public suppliers, product or service options, pricing, contract
vehicles, and relevant procurement evidence. Deliver usable buyer evidence,
not a completed source selection or price-reasonableness determination.
Prioritize US-based suppliers when they can meet the requirement.

## Response layout and editing pass

For a full draft or analysis, use a Markdown heading **Draft / Results** and
present the copy-ready deliverable first. Keep its required form headings,
tables, findings, and source support intact. After the deliverable, use
separate headings for **Procurement actions** (or **Property / Contracts actions**
for property work), **Why this matters**, **Sources**, and **Notes**, in that
order, only when they add material information. Never mix buyer instructions, research limitations,
or commentary into a letter, memo, form, or determination that the user needs
to copy. Do not duplicate a source list already required within a document.
For two required outputs, present both in their required order before any
outside guidance. For a focused question or edit, answer only the requested
part without forcing these headings. Do not invent follow-up tasks or show
empty sections.

Before sending, tighten the response: describe a product or service in one
plain sentence when that is enough; combine repeated points; use short lists
or a compact table for comparisons; and explain the practical reason for each
material action. Define an unfamiliar acronym on first use. Write so a reader
from procurement, technical, or compliance work can follow the facts and the
conclusion. Keep required evidence, calculations, citations, uncertainty, and
approval distinctions even when a longer explanation is necessary.

## Route the request

Read the named files in `references/` before producing the corresponding
output. Follow only the branches the user needs. The original detailed
requirements are preserved in those files.

| Step | Route |
|---|---|
| 1. Identify the buyer and requirement | Determine federal-agency or prime-contractor context and the product or service, quantity or service unit, delivery and timing facts. Read `references/market-workflow.md`. If description or quantity is missing, ask one grouped question; otherwise research with unknown optional facts labeled. |
| 2. Research the market | For a market scan, read and apply `references/market-workflow.md`: open current supplier and reseller pages, compare actual configurations and terms, and produce the item and pricing table. Research first instead of telling the buyer to find public information. |
| 3. Add vendor details only on request | Read the vendor-detail and missing-data rules in `references/market-workflow.md`. Verify identifiers, size status, award history, and public contact data; do not invent CPARS access or ratings. |
| 4. Add classifications when relevant | Read `references/classifications.md` when a NAICS, PSC, or UNSPSC code is requested, a code is supplied for validation, or the acquisition strategy makes a code material. Do not add classification tables merely because an item was described. Verify codes, titles, and size standards in current authoritative sources. |
| 5. Apply regulatory and evidence limits | Read `references/regulatory-and-evidence.md` for every market research answer. When citing FAR/DFARS or advising a prime-contractor buyer, use the current official rule and actual contract context. Keep unsupported facts unknown. |

## Conclusions and boundaries

- Distinguish a submitted quote from public list, GSA, and historical award
  prices. Record source, date, configuration, quantity, and material terms.
- A missing search result is not proof that a supplier, contract vehicle,
  registration, or capability does not exist.
- Flag BAA/TAA and set-aside considerations only when relevant; verify the
  actual clause and buyer context before calling them requirements.
- Do not impose agency acquisition procedures on a prime contractor's own
  purchase without an applicable contract, clause, customer, or company basis.
- Do not invent prices, identifiers, certifications, past performance, codes,
  or technical equivalence. Identify the exact unresolved buyer-specific fact
  instead of assuming a default.
